Tankless water heater installation in Houston

A tankless heater gives you hot water on demand and frees up the space a tank takes. Whether it's right for your home comes down to your gas line, your venting and how much hot water you use at the same time.

[[cta]]

At a glance

  • Tankless units can last more than 20 years, compared with 10–15 for most tanks (U.S. Department of Energy).
  • They're sized by flow rate in gallons per minute, not by gallons stored.
  • Converting from a tank usually means gas line, venting or electrical work.
  • In the City of Houston, it needs a plumbing permit and an inspection, like any water heater.

Sizing: think flow rate, not gallons

A tankless heater is rated by how many gallons per minute it can heat, and by how many degrees it can raise the temperature at that flow. Add up what might run at the same time. A shower uses roughly 2 to 2.5 gallons per minute, so a busy morning with two showers and a dishwasher needs a lot more than one shower does.

Size for winter. Incoming water is coldest in January, so the unit has to lift it further. A plumber should size it for a cold morning, not a summer afternoon. If several showers often run at once, the answer may be a larger unit or two units working together.

What converting from a tank involves

  • Gas supply. A whole-house gas tankless unit burns far more gas at full fire than a typical tank heater. The gas line, and sometimes the meter, has to be checked for capacity.
  • Venting. Tankless units need their own vent, usually a different type than the old tank's flue. Condensing models also need a drain for condensate.
  • Electrical. Gas units need an outlet for their controls and freeze protection. Electric whole-house units can need a very large electrical service, sometimes more than a home has.
  • Water quality. Scale is a tankless heater's main enemy. Your plumber may add service valves for easy flushing, and may suggest a scale filter or softener.
  • Permit and inspection. Houston treats it like any water heater replacement: a permit pulled by a registered master plumber, then an inspection.
  • Placement. Moving the heater out of the attic or garage can free space, but longer pipe runs mean a longer wait for hot water at distant taps.

Is tankless a good fit for your home?

Often a good fit if…

  • You plan to stay in the home for years.
  • You regularly run out of hot water with a tank.
  • You want the floor space back, or the heater out of the attic.
  • Your gas service is adequate, or a plumber confirms the upgrade is reasonable.

Maybe not if…

  • Your current tank is young and working well.
  • Gas, venting or electrical upgrades would cost more than you'd save over time.
  • Several showers often run at once and the budget only covers one unit.
  • You're selling the house soon.

Tankless questions

Do tankless water heaters need maintenance?

Yes. Minerals in the water build up as scale in the heat exchanger, so flush or descale the unit on the schedule in its manual and clean the inlet screen. Skipping it shortens the unit's life and can void the warranty.

Will a gas tankless heater work during a power outage?

Usually not. Most gas tankless units need electricity for their controls, fan and ignition, unlike many standard gas tanks. If outages worry you, ask about backup power for the unit.

Can a tankless heater go outside?

Many models are made for outdoor mounting, which saves indoor space and venting. They rely on built-in electric freeze protection, so a freeze combined with a power outage needs attention. Follow the manufacturer's draining instructions.

Does a tankless heater need a permit in Houston?

Yes. Inside City of Houston limits, installing any water heater, tankless included, requires a plumbing permit pulled by a master plumber registered with the City, followed by an inspection.
