Water heater leaking? Do this right now

Most leaks can be stopped in a few minutes with a switch and a valve. Then you can work out whether it's a quick repair or a replacement.

[[cta]]

If you smell gas

Leave the house now. Don't flip switches, use phones or do anything else that could make a spark. From a safe place outside, call 911 and CenterPoint Energy's gas leak line at 713-659-2111 or 800-752-8036.

Stop the leak in four steps

  1. Stay clear of the hot water. Water in the tank can be hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and keep your hands out of the stream.
  2. Turn off the energy. For an electric heater, switch off its breaker in the electrical panel. If the floor near the panel is wet, don't touch it. Wait for a professional. For a gas heater, turn the gas control knob to OFF, or close the gas valve on the supply pipe so the handle sits crosswise to the pipe.
  3. Shut off the cold-water supply. Find the valve on the cold inlet pipe at the top of the heater. Turn a round handle clockwise, or a lever a quarter turn. If it won't close, shut off the main water valve to the house.
  4. Contain the water. Use towels, buckets or a wet vacuum. For an attic heater, check the ceiling below for stains or sagging, and keep everyone out from under it.

Then call [[call-link]], day or night. We'll pass your request to {{partnerCompany}}, a licensed plumbing company.

Then call a licensed plumber. You can check any Texas plumber's license at tsbpe.texas.gov.

Where is the water coming from?

Pipes or fittings on top

Loose or corroded connections on the hot and cold lines. Usually repairable.

The relief valve or its pipe

The temperature and pressure relief valve opens when the tank gets too hot or the pressure gets too high. A drip can mean a worn valve, or a real temperature or pressure problem. Never cap or plug it. Turn the heater off and have a plumber check it.

The drain valve near the bottom

A drip from the drain valve's spout often means the valve isn't fully closed or is worn. Usually repairable.

The tank itself

Water seeping from under the outer jacket, or a puddle with no other source, usually means the inner tank has rusted through. Needs replacement.

Before blaming the heater, rule out nearby sources: an air conditioner or furnace condensate line, a water softener, or a supply line to another appliance.

Leak questions

Can I keep using a leaking water heater?

Not if the tank itself is leaking. Leave the water and the energy off until a plumber has looked at it. A slow drip from a fitting is less urgent, but have it checked soon. Small leaks tend to grow, and water under an attic unit can damage a ceiling before you notice.

Why is water coming out of the relief valve pipe?

The valve releases water when temperature or pressure in the tank gets too high. It can also leak when it's worn. Either way, don't block it. Turn the heater off and call a plumber, because a valve that can't open is a safety hazard.

Do I need a whole new water heater?

If the tank has rusted through, yes. Leaks from fittings, the drain valve or the relief valve can often be repaired. If the heater is more than about 10 years old, replacing it may make more sense than fixing it. Signs it's time to replace.
