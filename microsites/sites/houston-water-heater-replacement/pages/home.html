Repair or replace? Signs it's time for a new water heater

It's 10 or more years old

The Department of Energy puts the typical life of a tank water heater at 10–15 years. The install date is usually on the label or coded into the serial number. Near the end of that range, a big repair rarely pays off.

Water is leaking from the tank

Drips from a fitting, the drain valve or the relief valve can often be fixed. Water seeping from the tank body, or pooling under it with no other source, usually means the inner tank has corroded through. That can't be repaired.

Rusty or smelly hot water

Rust-colored water from the hot taps only can mean the tank is corroding inside. A rotten-egg smell usually comes from bacteria reacting with the tank's anode rod. A plumber can often fix the smell by replacing the rod.

Rumbling, popping, or running out fast

Minerals settle at the bottom of the tank as sediment. It rumbles, wastes energy and cuts how much hot water you get. Flushing helps when it's caught early. A tank that's badly scaled is usually ready to retire.

What a proper Houston replacement includes

A water heater swap looks simple, but it involves gas or high-amperage wiring, scalding water and pressure. This is what to expect from a licensed plumber in the City of Houston.

  • A city permit and inspection. Houston requires a plumbing permit to replace or install a water heater. A master plumber registered with the City has to pull it, and the work is inspected.
  • The right size, not just the same size. Tank heaters are sized by first-hour rating: how much hot water they deliver in your busiest hour. Gallons alone, or whatever was there before, aren't enough to go on.
  • A working temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe that drains where hot water can't hurt anyone.
  • A drain pan where a leak could do damage. That's typical for attic and upstairs closet installs.
  • Expansion control if your plumbing needs it. A home with a pressure-reducing valve or backflow preventer may need a thermal expansion tank to meet code.
  • Safe gas and venting for gas units: a shutoff valve, a sediment trap and a vent that draws properly.
  • Haul-away of the old heater and a thermostat setting of about 120°F, which the Department of Energy suggests for most homes.
  • A written proposal and invoice. Texas rules require them to show the Responsible Master Plumber's name and license number, plus the state plumbing board's contact details.

Tank, tankless, gas or electric?

Gas tank

A common choice where there's gas service. It recovers quickly and costs the least up front of the gas options. It needs proper venting and combustion air.

Electric tank

Simple to install where there's no gas line, but slower to recover than gas. Heat pump models use far less electricity, but they need space and airflow around them.

Tankless

Heats water on demand, so there's no tank to run out of or to rust. It costs more to install and often needs a bigger gas line and new venting. Is tankless right for you?

Houston details that change the job

  • Attics, closets and garages. Houston homes are mostly built on slabs without basements, so water heaters sit in garages, closets and attics. An attic leak can ruin a ceiling fast. Drain pans and easy-to-reach shutoff valves matter here.
  • A flooded heater should be checked before it's used. Gas water heaters carry a warning not to use them if any part has been under water. After a flood, have a licensed plumber inspect the heater instead of relighting it. Usually it has to be replaced.
  • Inside or outside city limits? Plenty of "Houston" mailing addresses are in unincorporated Harris County or in neighboring cities, where permit rules differ. Your plumber should confirm which rules apply to your address.
  • Hard freezes happen. Outdoor tankless units depend on electric freeze protection. If a freeze comes with a power outage, follow the manufacturer's instructions for draining the unit.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Houston?

Yes. Inside City of Houston limits, replacing or installing a water heater requires a plumbing permit. A master plumber registered with the City has to pull it, and the job is inspected. For a single-family home, the City's minimum permit fee plus its admin fee comes to under $135 (2026 rates). Outside city limits, the rules differ.

How long does a replacement take?

A straightforward like-for-like tank swap usually takes a few hours once the plumber is on site. Switching fuel types, converting to tankless or adding code upgrades can stretch it to a full day or more. The city inspection is scheduled separately.

How much does a new water heater cost?

It depends on the type and fuel, the size, where the heater sits (attics take more labor), the code work your setup needs, and whether it's after hours. Ask for an itemized written quote so you can compare like with like. The state plumbing board doesn't regulate plumbers' prices, so getting more than one quote is reasonable.

Should I repair my water heater instead?

Repair often makes sense for a younger heater with one failed part, such as a thermostat, heating element, thermocouple or gas valve, or a leaking fitting. Replacement makes sense when the tank itself leaks, when the heater is past about 10 years old, or when repairs keep adding up.
