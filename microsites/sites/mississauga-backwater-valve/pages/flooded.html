Basement flooded? Do this right now

Safety first, then stop the backup from getting worse, then record the damage for your insurer before you clean up.

[[cta]]

Water near electricity?

If water has reached outlets, baseboard heaters, the furnace or the electrical panel, don't go into the basement. Call Alectra Utilities at 1-833-253-2872 (option 1) and wait until the power is disconnected. If anyone is in danger, call 911.

Smell gas? Leave the house, then call Enbridge Gas at 1-866-763-5427 or 911 from a safe distance.

What to do, in order

  1. Stay safe. Water conducts electricity, and floodwater can carry sewage and bacteria. Wear rubber boots and gloves, and keep children and pets out.
  2. If sewage is coming up a floor drain, stop using water. No flushing, showers, laundry or dishwasher. Every litre you send down the drain has nowhere to go.
  3. Report it. For flooding on roads or in your neighbourhood, call 311. If you think the problem is in the sanitary sewer, use Peel Region's emergency help with a sewer backup request.
  4. Call your insurer immediately. Take photos and video of the water and the damage before you move or throw anything out.
  5. Clean up safely. Pump out standing water, and have a restoration contractor deal with finishes and contents that got wet. If water stood for a long time, Peel recommends removing materials at least 50 cm (20 in) above the high-water line to prevent mould. Throw away any food that touched floodwater.
  6. Before the power goes back on, have a licensed electrical contractor check anything electrical that got wet.

Then call [[call-link]], day or night, about protecting the basement next time. We'll pass your request to {{partnerCompany}}, a licensed plumbing company.

Once you're dry, talk to a licensed plumber about protecting the basement next time. You can check any Ontario plumber on the Skilled Trades Ontario public register.

Where did the water come from?

Up through the floor drain

Dirty water rising from the drain during a storm usually means the sewer backed up. A backwater valve is the fix, and Peel rebates 60% of a sanitary one, up to $1,500.

Through the walls or the floor edges

Water seeping in where the walls meet the floor often comes from the weeping tile or the soil around the foundation. A sump pump, disconnected downspouts or better grading help, and the City rebates up to $7,500.

Windows and cracks

Window wells and foundation cracks let water in during heavy rain. A valve won't stop this. Sealing, window well covers and grading will.

A burst pipe or appliance

Clean water from a supply line, water heater or washer is a plumbing repair. Shut off the main water valve and call a plumber.

Peel also suggests checking that your home insurance includes "sewer back-up" or "broad water" coverage. Not every policy does. Rebates for prevention work.
