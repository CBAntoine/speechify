Sump pump installation in Mississauga

A sump pump collects water from the weeping tile around your foundation and pumps it out to your lawn, instead of sending it down a pipe to the City's storm sewer. The City of Mississauga pays up to $6,000 toward one, as long as it's installed the City's way.

[[cta]]

At a glance

  • Up to $6,000 from the City for a sump pump connected to your existing weeping tile.
  • Also covered: up to $1,000 to cap the old foundation drain pipe, and up to $500 for downspout disconnections.
  • The City must pre-approve the work before it starts.

What a rebate-eligible install involves

  1. Pit and pump

    The weeping tile around the foundation is connected to a new sump pit. The pump lifts that water to the surface, away from the house.

  2. Cap the old connection

    The existing foundation drain pipe to the City's storm sewer is capped or disconnected. This needs a building permit.

  3. Disconnect downspouts

    Downspouts come off the weeping tile and drain at least 4 to 6 feet from the foundation, onto a lawn, a garden or into a rain barrel.

Where the water goes matters. The City says discharge from sump pumps and downspouts must not harm neighbouring properties, parks, schools or the municipal right-of-way, and must not drain straight into a watercourse.

Backup power and a second pump

A sump pump only works while it has power, and storms knock power out. The City won't pay for a secondary or backup pump, or for generators or backup power that weren't part of the original install. If you want battery backup, raise it with your plumber before you apply for pre-approval, so it's in the quote from the start.

What the City's rebate doesn't cover

Interior waterproofing systems

Sump pumps tied into internal weeping tile or waterproofing systems aren't eligible. Only pumps connected to the existing foundation drain are.

New builds and infill

Sump pumps required as part of new or infill development don't qualify.

Repairs and restoration

Weeping tile repair, eavestrough work, drywall, paint, flooring, landscaping, decks and driveways are all excluded.

Your own labour

Work you do yourself to lower the bill isn't reimbursed. The installer has to be a qualified, licensed contractor.

Sump pump questions

Do I need a permit?

The City requires a building permit to cap the foundation drain pipe, which is part of a rebate-eligible sump pump install, and the inspection has to be signed off before final approval. Your plumber should handle the application.

I live in a condo townhouse. Can I still apply?

Condo townhomes often share foundation drains. The City requires a consultation with City staff and authorization from the condo board before an application. Call 311 (905-615-4311 outside city limits) to ask for a basement flooding prevention rebate consultation.

What if my foundation drain goes to the sanitary sewer?

That's a non-standard connection. The City says to consult the Region of Peel before you submit a rebate application.

How do I know the pump still works?

Check it before the wet seasons, following the manufacturer's instructions. A common test is to pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the pump switch on and empty it. The City makes the homeowner, not the City, responsible for maintaining equipment installed under the program.
