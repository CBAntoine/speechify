Mississauga basement flooding rebates in 2026

Two programs pay toward flood protection here: the Region of Peel's sanitary backwater valve rebate and the City of Mississauga's basement flooding prevention rebate. They cover different work and have different steps, and doing things in the wrong order can cost you the rebate.

[[cta]]

At a glance

  • Region of Peel: 60% of a sanitary backwater valve, up to $1,500. Apply after the install passes inspection.
  • City of Mississauga: up to $7,500 for a sump pump, storm-side work and downspouts. You must be pre-approved before any work starts.
  • Both need a licensed contractor, and the City permit and inspection where required.

Region of Peel: sanitary backwater valve rebate

Peel Region pays 60% of the invoiced amount, up to $1,500 including tax, toward a backwater valve on your sanitary pipe: the one that carries sewage from your toilets and drains.

Who qualifies

  • A single, semi or townhome in Peel, with the valve installed after July 16, 2024.
  • The applicant is an individual, not a company.
  • The valve is on a sanitary pipe that serves only one dwelling unit. Homes where a basement apartment or other units share the pipe aren't eligible, because that wouldn't meet the Ontario Building Code.
  • A licensed contractor confirms the valve suits the house and installs it.
  • There's a City building permit, and the install passes inspection.
  • One rebate per home every 10 years.
  • Peel strongly recommends disconnecting downspouts from its sanitary sewer. If you choose not to, you have to sign an acknowledgement and release form.

Steps

  1. Apply for a City of Mississauga building permit before installing.
  2. Have the valve installed and pass the City inspection.
  3. Only then, send Peel the application form and documents by email or mail.

Peel says it reviews applications within 5 business days and mails a cheque 4 to 6 weeks after approval. Questions: 905-791-7800 ext. 4409.

City of Mississauga: basement flooding prevention rebate

The City pays up to $7,500 toward protection on the storm side: a sump pump that takes over from the old foundation drain connection, or a backwater valve on the storm pipe.

Who qualifies

  • An individual who is the registered owner of a detached house, semi, duplex or townhouse in Mississauga.
  • No outstanding taxes, fees or charges owed to the City, and no by-law violations at the property.
  • No City sump pump rebate in the last 10 years.
  • The work is done by a qualified contractor licensed in their trade in Ontario.
  • Condo townhomes need a consultation with City staff and the condo board's authorization first. If your foundation drain connects to the sanitary sewer, talk to the Region of Peel before applying.
MeasureMaximum rebate
Sump pump (connected to the existing weeping tile)$6,000
Capping the foundation drain pipe to the storm sewer$1,000
Downspout disconnection$125 each, up to $500
Backwater valve on the storm pipe (downspouts must also be disconnected)$1,500

The total is capped at $7,500. Labour, materials and taxes count, and so do consulting or camera (CCTV) inspection costs used to plan the work. Money left over on one measure can't be moved to another.

Steps

  1. Pre-approval, before any work. This has been required since February 12, 2025. Send the City your tax bill, an itemized quote with the contractor's name and licence number, the contractor's sketch of the work, and the building permit for any foundation drain capping or storm backwater valve. Then wait for the City's email.
  2. Install and inspect. Use the same contractor as on the quote, and get the building permit inspection signed off.
  3. Final approval within six months of pre-approval. Submit the original paid-in-full invoice and the inspection sign-off. The City says cheques take 8 to 10 weeks.

Rebates are paid in the order complete applications arrive, until the money runs out or the program ends. Questions: floodrebate@mississauga.ca or 311 (905-615-4311 outside city limits).

Mistakes that cost people their rebate

Starting before pre-approval

The City only pays for work it pre-approved. Starting first, even in an emergency, can mean no City rebate.

No permit, no inspection

Both programs depend on the City's building permit and inspection sign-off. Get the permit before the floor is opened.

A shared sewer pipe

A valve on a sanitary pipe shared with a basement apartment doesn't meet the Building Code, so Peel won't rebate it. Each unit needs its own.

Paperwork that doesn't match

The City wants the final invoice from the same contractor as the pre-approved quote, with their licence number on it. A contractor who can't provide that is a red flag.

Work the City won't pay for

Your own labour, a backup or second sump pump, backup power added after the original install, pumps tied into interior waterproofing systems, weeping tile repair, and restoring drywall, flooring or landscaping.

Waiting for the next storm

Rebates are first-come, first-served while funding lasts, and permits and pre-approval take time. Planning in a dry month beats scrambling after a flood.
