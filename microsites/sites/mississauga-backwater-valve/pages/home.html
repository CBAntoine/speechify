Backwater valve installation in Mississauga

A backwater valve stops sewage from backing up through your basement floor drain when the sewer overloads in a storm. Here's how it works, what the Region of Peel and City of Mississauga rebates pay for, and what a proper install involves.

How a backwater valve works

  1. Normal days

    The valve's flap stays open, so water from your toilets, showers and sinks flows out to the sewer as usual.

  2. The sewer overloads

    Intense rain or a blockage can push sewage back toward your house. The flap closes on its own and blocks it.

  3. While it's closed

    Don't use toilets, sinks, showers, the dishwasher or the washer. The water has nowhere to go and can flood the basement from inside. Once the sewer drains, the flap reopens.

Peel Region suggests choosing a model with an alarm, so you know when the flap has closed.

Sanitary or storm? It decides which rebate you get

The sanitary pipe carries sewage from your toilets and drains to the Region of Peel's wastewater sewer. Some Mississauga homes also have a storm pipe that carries water from the weeping tile around the foundation to the City's storm sewer. A valve can go on either one, and each is covered by a different program.

Is a backwater valve right for your home?

It's a job for a professional

Installing one means cutting into the basement floor and the main sewer pipe. Peel Region is blunt about it: this is not a home improvement project.

One valve per dwelling unit

Under the Ontario Building Code, a valve can only go on a sanitary pipe that serves one dwelling unit. A basement apartment needs its own. Homes where units share a sewer pipe don't qualify for Peel's rebate.

It won't stop every flood

A backwater valve blocks sewer backups. It doesn't stop water coming through windows, foundation cracks or weeping tile. Those need other fixes, such as a sump pump or grading.

It needs a yearly check

Peel recommends inspecting and cleaning the valve at least once a year, or before forecast heavy rain, so debris doesn't stop the flap from sealing.

What a proper Mississauga install includes

This is what to expect from a licensed plumber, and what both rebate programs check for.

  • A City building permit before work starts. Apply in ePlans with no drawings needed. There's a $50 admin fee, and the permit is usually issued in about three business days.
  • The right pipe, confirmed. Sanitary or storm, and whether a basement unit shares the line, settled before anyone breaks concrete.
  • A certified plumber doing the work. Plumbing is a compulsory trade in Ontario: the people doing it must hold a certificate or be registered apprentices, and you can look them up on the Skilled Trades Ontario public register.
  • An access cover over the valve so it can be opened and cleaned every year.
  • A City inspection after the install. Once it's signed off, the permit status reads "Completed – All inspections signed off". Both rebates rely on it.
  • An itemized quote and invoice showing the work, the cost breakdown, and the contractor's name and licence number. The City's rebate requires them.
  • Downspouts disconnected, draining 4 to 6 feet away from the foundation. Peel strongly recommends it, and the City requires it for storm-side rebates.
  • Advice on the rest of your risk: grading, foundation cracks and whether a sump pump makes sense.

What about a sump pump?

If water seeps in around the foundation rather than up through the floor drain, a sump pump is often the answer. It collects weeping tile water and pumps it out to your lawn. The City of Mississauga's rebate pays up to $6,000 toward a sump pump installed its way. How a rebate-eligible sump pump install works.

Do I need a permit for a backwater valve in Mississauga?

Yes. The City requires a building permit for every backwater valve, sanitary or storm. You apply online in ePlans, no drawings are needed, the fee is $50, and the permit is usually issued in about three business days. After the install, book a plumbing inspection.

How much does a backwater valve cost?

It depends on how deep the pipe is, the floor you're cutting through, whether a camera inspection is needed and what has to be restored afterward. Ask for an itemized written quote. Can I get both rebates?

They pay for different work. Peel's covers a valve on the sanitary pipe. The City's covers sump pumps and storm-side work, including a valve on the storm pipe. Each has its own rules, and the City's needs pre-approval before work starts. Depending on your pipes, a home may qualify for one, the other or both. Compare the programs.
